# USPAP + Fannie citations — VERIFIED, use these exact ones

Current edition: **2024 USPAP**, effective 2024-01-01. There is no 2026 edition.
Verified 2026-08-18 against the USPAP text and the Nebraska Real Property
Appraiser Board's 2024-2025 USPAP Compliance Review Report Form (which prints
the SR 2-2 list verbatim).

A wrong citation is worse than no citation. Do not paraphrase these from memory.

---

## Highest and best use

| Duty | Rule |
|---|---|
| Identify AND analyze the effect on use and on value of: existing land use regulations, reasonably probable modifications of them, economic supply and demand, physical adaptability, market area trends | **SR 1-3(a)** |
| Develop an opinion of the highest and best use of the real estate | **SR 1-3(b)** |
| **Appraisal Report** (the 1004): state the opinion **and summarize the support and rationale** for it | **SR 2-2(a)(xii)** |
| **Restricted Appraisal Report**: state the opinion only — no support required | **SR 2-2(b)(xiv)** |

### ⚠ CORRECTION — his current boilerplate cites the wrong subparagraph

His template says **2-2(a)(x)**. That is wrong in the 2024 edition.
**SR 2-2(a)(x)** is the STANDARD 1 compliance catch-all — methods and techniques,
reasons for excluding an approach, the SR 1-5 sales-history results, stating the
value opinion, and the reconciliation reasoning. It says nothing about highest
and best use.

Highest and best use is **SR 2-2(a)(xii)**.

History of this error, so it does not come back:
- The **original** boilerplate cited 2-2(b)(x) — wrong report type *and* a weaker
  duty (Restricted only requires the opinion be stated).
- The 2026-08-12 fix moved it to 2-2(a)(x) — right report type, still the wrong
  roman numeral. The confusion is old: pre-2014 editions of USPAP really did have
  H&BU at 2-2(a)(x), and stale web articles still print that list.
- Correct as of the 2024 edition: **2-2(a)(xii)**.

## Exposure time

| Duty | Rule |
|---|---|
| When reasonable exposure time is a component of the definition of value being developed, **develop** an opinion of reasonable exposure time **linked to that value opinion** | **SR 1-2(c), Comment** |
| When that opinion has been developed under SR 1-2(c), it **must be stated in the report** | **SR 2-2(a)(vi), Comment** (Restricted: SR 2-2(b)(viii), Comment) |
| Guidance | **Advisory Opinion 35**, *Reasonable Exposure Time in Real Property and Personal Property Opinions of Value* |

Note the verb: **stated**, not summarized. USPAP itself is satisfied by the
number. The support is a workfile matter and a client expectation — which is
exactly why the support belongs in the workfile document and the number plus a
short basis belongs on the form.

Definition (2024 USPAP): exposure time is "the estimated length of time that the
property interest being appraised would have been offered on the market prior to
the hypothetical consummation of a sale at market value on the effective date of
the appraisal." **Retrospective.** It ends at the effective date.

## Marketing time

| Point | Authority |
|---|---|
| An opinion of the time it might take to sell at the concluded market value during the period **immediately after** the effective date | **Advisory Opinion 7**, *Marketing Time Opinions* |
| **Not required by USPAP.** It becomes an assignment condition only when the client asks for it | AO-7 |

**Prospective.** It begins at the effective date. Exposure time looks backward,
marketing time looks forward. Never state one range and label it both without
saying why they are the same.

## Fannie Mae — Neighborhood section (B4-1.3-03)

- Boundaries must be outlined using **North, South, East, West**, referencing
  streets, legally recognized boundaries, waterways, or other natural features.
- One-unit housing trends use a **minimum of 12 months** of data: property values
  (increasing / stable / declining), supply (shortage / in balance / over supply),
  marketing time (under 3 months / 3-6 months / over 6 months).
- Declining values, over-supply, **or marketing time over six months** → the
  appraiser **must state the reasons**.
- Present land use percentages: one-unit and 2-4 unit reported separately,
  undeveloped land noted separately. Percentages total 100.
- Predominant price and predominant age are each a **single whole number** — the
  most frequently found value, not an average.
- Explain when the subject's age falls outside the stated range.
- Protected characteristics — sex, race, color, religion, disability, national
  origin, familial status — must not be considered in the valuation or the
  reporting. The form itself prints "Race and the racial composition of the
  neighborhood are not appraisal factors."

## Fannie Mae — Highest and best use

Fannie presumes existing residential improvements represent the highest and best
use "until it becomes financially feasible to redevelop the site"
(**B4-1.3-04**). A tight report narrative plus a documented workfile satisfies
both Fannie and SR 2-2(a)(xii).

## Fannie Mae — Market conditions / time adjustments

Effective **March 1, 2025**, market change must be measured **from the contract
date of each comparable to the effective date**, not the closing date. The report
must carry a summary of the supporting evidence and a description of the data
sources, tools and techniques. Failure to make market-derived time adjustments
when the data indicates them is an unacceptable appraisal practice.
