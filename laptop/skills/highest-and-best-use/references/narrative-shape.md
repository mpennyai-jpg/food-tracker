# Narrative shape and voice — templates only

**Nothing in this file is data.** Every figure is a bracketed placeholder. If a
bracket survives into a finished document, the document is not finished.

These templates carry sentence rhythm, ordering, and length. They carry no facts.

---

## REPORT narrative — DEFAULT: conclusion-only, ~90 words (his call, 2026-08-24)

On a standard residential lot with a clean pass on all four tests, use this
form. Nothing below it in this file applies unless an adverse finding forces
the longer treatment (see SKILL.md step 7 for the carve-out).

```
HIGHEST AND BEST USE  (report narrative)

I analyzed the subject's zoning, site characteristics, market conditions,
and alternative uses under the four tests of highest and best use — legal
permissibility, physical possibility, financial feasibility, and maximum
productivity. Continued use of the existing improvements passes all four
tests. Highest and best use as improved is the present use: [CONFIGURATION
— e.g. one single family detached dwelling on one lot]. As if vacant,
highest and best use is construction of a single family residence. This
site holds no surplus land and no excess land. My workfile holds the full
analysis, including review of [THE REAL QUESTIONS THIS ASSIGNMENT RAISED —
e.g. the site size and the alternative uses permitted by zoning].
```

Fill the two brackets from this assignment. The closing clause names what
the workfile reviewed so a reader knows the questions were analyzed without
being handed the analysis.

## REPORT narrative — LONGER FORM, only when an adverse finding must go on the form

Structure, in this order:

1. Zoning district and what it permits by right, then the subject's compliance
2. Lot area and width against the district minimums
3. The division test — **only if the site invites the question**
4. Feasibility finding and the conclusion, stated as a configuration
5. Surplus and excess land, one sentence
6. The workfile pointer

```
HIGHEST AND BEST USE  (report narrative)

[JURISDICTION] zones this site [DISTRICT], [DISTRICT NAME]. [DISTRICT] permits
[PERMITTED USE] by right, and subject use complies as [COMPLIANCE FINDING].
Site holds [AREA] square feet and fronts [WIDTH] feet, against minimums of
[MIN AREA] square feet and [MIN WIDTH] feet.

[DIVISION PARAGRAPH — include only when site area meaningfully exceeds the
minimum. Two sentences. State the multiple of the minimum, then why each
division fails: one on width or area, one on frontage.]

Buyers pay far more for this property improved than the land brings vacant, and
building new here costs more than a finished house sells for. Present use is
highest and best use: [CONFIGURATION — e.g. one single-family detached dwelling
on one lot]. This site holds [no surplus land and no excess land / SURPLUS OR
EXCESS FINDING]. My workfile holds the full analysis.
```

**The division paragraph is the judgment call.** Include it when a reviewer would
ask "could this be split?" — roughly 1.75x the minimum area and up, or any time
the lender raised the site size. Drop it entirely on an ordinary conforming lot.

## Voice patterns that work

Rejecting a permitted alternative use — evidence, not assertion:

> [DISTRICT] also allows [ALTERNATIVE USE] as a special exception at [STANDARD],
> which this site could hold in theory. I rejected that option. Subject sits among
> [N] [PROPERTY TYPE] on lots of similar size. No [ALTERNATIVE USE] development
> exists in this market area, and no sale or listing in my search supports one. A
> special exception carries approval risk and no demonstrated demand.

Financial feasibility, developer's test:

> Constructing the dwelling new costs [COST NEW]. Add site value of [SITE VALUE]
> and the total reaches [TOTAL], before any driveway, walks, or landscaping.
> Market value stands at [VALUE]. Those figures leave [RESIDUAL] for builder
> profit, so no builder would put this dwelling on this lot today.

Financial feasibility, absorption:

> My [N] sales sold in [DAYS LIST] days. I researched [N] sales in this market
> area. [Demand/supply finding], and typical marketing time runs [PERIOD].

Maximally productive, with the comparison shown:

> Compare two options. Keep the dwelling, and the property returns [VALUE]. Clear
> the site, and it returns [SITE VALUE] before demolition cost. Removing the
> dwelling would destroy roughly [DIFFERENCE] in contributory value.

Surplus and excess, stated even when negative:

> I measured site size against what this market requires, not against the zoning
> minimum. My comparable sales carry sites from [LOW] to [HIGH]. Subject site of
> [AREA] falls [POSITION]. This site holds no excess land, because [REASON]. It
> holds no surplus land either, because [REASON].

## Voice rules these templates encode

Short sentences. One idea each. First person. Active voice. **No sentence starts
with "The"** — note every template above obeys this. Terms of art kept, sentences
around them simplified. No adjective without a number behind it.

Read the draft back and fix any sentence that breaks one of those.
