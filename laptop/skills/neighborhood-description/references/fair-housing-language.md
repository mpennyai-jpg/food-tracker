# Neighborhood language — what must never appear

The 1004 prints the rule on the form itself: **"Race and the racial composition
of the neighborhood are not appraisal factors."**

Fannie B4-1.3-03 is broader. Protected characteristics — **sex, race, color,
religion, disability, national origin, familial status** — must not be considered
in the valuation or in the reporting. This is the single highest-exposure area in
the whole form. A sentence that reads as harmless can end a license.

Run this list against every neighborhood paragraph before writing it out.

## Never write — direct references

Any mention of the race, color, religion, national origin, ethnicity, sex,
disability status, or familial status of residents. Any mention of the
neighborhood's composition in those terms. This includes flattering versions.

## Never write — proxies

These are the ones that slip through, because each sounds like market analysis:

| Do not write | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "desirable neighborhood", "prestigious", "pride of ownership" | Subjective, unmeasurable, and historically used as a proxy |
| "good schools", "poor schools", school ratings or test scores | Proxy for composition. Name schools as facilities that exist and stop. |
| crime statistics, "safe", "high crime", "rough area" | Proxy, and outside his competency to source |
| "student area", "family neighborhood", "retirement community", "starter homes for young families" | Familial status and age proxies |
| "up and coming", "transitional", "gentrifying", "declining area" | Composition-change language |
| "affordable to working families", "low income area" | Proxy, and unnecessary — price data says it factually |
| "close to church", "walkable to the synagogue", "near the mosque" | Religion proxy. Name a corridor, not a house of worship. |
| "language spoken", signage language, business names as character evidence | National origin proxy |

## What to write instead — factual, measurable, sourced

- Structure types, architectural styles, typical site size, street pattern
- Age range and price range of one-unit housing, with the data behind them
- Land use percentages
- Named routes and travel access — "[ROUTE] provides regional access"
- Employment, retail and public facilities as things that exist at a distance —
  "Retail and public services sit along [NAMED CORRIDOR]"
- Utilities, flood zone, topography
- Measured market conditions — supply, absorption, days on market, price trend
- External influences stated physically: a rail line, an interstate, an industrial
  use, a landfill, a floodplain

The test: **could a reviewer verify this sentence from data?** If not, it does not
belong in the neighborhood section.

## Declining values, over-supply, marketing time over six months

Fannie requires the reasons when any of these three is checked. State them
economically — employment loss at a named employer, new construction competition,
a rate environment, an absorption rate — never demographically.

## One more, from his own file

His prior neighborhood paragraphs open *"The subject property is located..."* and
run passive. Both break the voice rules he was given by his mentor. Fix them:
never start a sentence with "The", and never write in passive voice.
