7. Comp Selection
Hierarchy — subdivision deals
1. Subdivision
2. Distance
3. Everything else
Hierarchy — one-off or rural deals
Distance doesn't govern. He's looking for the
correct comp.
"You never really ﬁnd the perfect comp."
Age of sale
Three years back is hard for him.
Even for a strong comp, he'll go back 18 months,
maybe 2 years.

Beyond that it would have to be the perfect comp
on a very weird deal.
Never to hit a number. "Not something just to try
to hit value or something
like that. Never that."
Distance commentary rule
Any comp beyond roughly 10 miles, where it isn't
obvious the deal is rural, gets
discussed in the report.
Example framing: went 20 miles for this comp
because it has an in-ground pool and
a workshop, matching the subject — then address
whether the two areas are
similar.
Bracketing discipline
Rarely lets anything go unbracketed.
Baths: will let it go if the subject is extreme (e.g.
4.1 baths with a pool of
4-bath comps is ﬁne).
Bedrooms: same — 5 or 6 bedrooms can go
unbracketed.
Bed placement: e.g. subject with 1 bedroom on
the main and 2 in the basement —
he'll try hard to bracket that.
Site size: bracket.

Square footage / GLA: do our best to bracket.
It would take the data simply not existing for him
to skip it.
8. Adjustments
Method
TruTracts runs regression analysis to ﬁnd what
the market actually pays for a
feature — e.g. what a bathroom really goes for.
Claude is used to back up and support
adjustments.
Core principle: depreciated cost is not an
adjustment. If it cost $28,000,
that's just what it cost. The adjustment has to
reﬂect what the market reacts to.
Figuring out the market reaction is the whole job.
Paired sales are nearly impossible to produce in
this business, but clients
always want them.
Sample sizes TruTracts pulls
Busy area (Rutherford County): ~1,200 sales.
Rural area (Lincoln County): ~200 sales.

Override
When the output doesn't match what he knows, he
overrides as the appraiser.
Example: TruTracts says $45,000 for a pool in
Lincoln County. Not happening —
that market doesn't pay for pools. Realistically
closer to $20,000.
Always case by case.
Documentation of overrides
Currently: not documented. "I don't. I should."
Flag: this is the real audit exposure. A sentence or two
in the workﬁle at the
moment the override is made would close the gap —
the commission's question will be
"where's your reasoning?"
