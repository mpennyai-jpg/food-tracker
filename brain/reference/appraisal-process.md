# Appraisal process, start to finish

One-line summary: Todd's workflow from bid to delivery, including the QC pass that jams most often.

## Intake

1. File comes in; Todd bids it. Sometimes the fee is already attached.
2. Once accepted, it goes into a folder.
3. Comps are pulled before going out to the property.

## Inspection

Photos of the exterior. Sketch and measure. Go inside. Talk to the homeowner if present. Photos of every room plus deficiencies, crawl space, attic. Check water, utilities, appliances. Everything goes into the mobile app on site.

## Back at the office

1. Download mobile data into a TOTAL form.
2. Pick comps.
3. Send to Laura for data entry.
4. Laura returns the file; Todd QCs it.
5. Adjustments.
6. Narratives, reconciliation, delivery.

## QC pass on Laura's return

Checks: page 1 correct, sales contract in, all MLS listings of the subject listed, map ran correctly, photos in and arranged, MLS data transferred correctly.

This pass, making sure everything is right and he feels comfortable with the appraisal, is where the file jams most often. Not waiting on Laura, not the adjustments. "It just seems to take so long."

## Writing

- Neighborhood description: Claude, from Claude plus TruTracts.
- Market conditions: same.
- Cost approach: Spark for Appraisers, basic commentary.
- Reconciliation: takes the longest, not yet moved to Claude. Justification and the closing reconciliation narrative are the biggest time sinks.

## Turn time

Nothing gets turned in early. Example: 5 properties inspected on a Saturday, all delivered within 6 days. Contractual AMC turn time is an open question.

## Revisions

About 2 here and there, usually nothing serious. Worst offenders in order: Clarity, Class, Service.
