# Adjustments and market conditions

One-line summary: regression-backed, market-reaction adjustments with appraiser override, and the documentation gap that is the real audit exposure.

## Method

- TruTracts runs regression to find what the market actually pays for a feature, such as what a bathroom really goes for.
- Claude backs up and supports adjustments.
- Core principle: depreciated cost is not an adjustment. If it cost $28,000, that is just what it cost. The adjustment has to reflect what the market reacts to. Figuring out the market reaction is the whole job.
- Paired sales are nearly impossible to produce, but clients always want them.

## Sample sizes TruTracts pulls

| Area | Sales |
|---|---|
| Rutherford County (busy) | ~1,200 |
| Lincoln County (rural) | ~200 |

## Override

When the output does not match what he knows, he overrides as the appraiser. Example: TruTracts says $45,000 for a pool in Lincoln County. That market does not pay for pools. Realistically closer to $20,000. Always case by case.

## Documentation of overrides

Currently not documented. "I don't. I should." This is the real audit exposure. A sentence or two in the workfile at the moment of the override closes the gap. The commission's question will be "where's your reasoning?"
